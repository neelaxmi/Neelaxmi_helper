NEET UG Guide

The Complete NEET UG Preparation Roadmap

A realistic, month-by-month plan for Physics, Chemistry, and Biology — built around how NEET is actually scored, not how a syllabus PDF is organized.

16 min read NEET UG

Every year, lakhs of NEET aspirants start their prep with the same 720-mark target and the same three subjects — and end up with wildly different outcomes. The difference is rarely raw intelligence. It's almost always sequencing: what you study first, how long you stay on each topic, and when you switch from learning mode to recall mode. This roadmap gives you that sequencing, broken down month by month, for a standard two-year-into-final-year timeline that you can compress if you're starting late.

Subject-Wise Weightage: Know the Battlefield First

Before you plan a single month, you need to know where marks actually come from. NEET is 720 marks across Physics (180), Chemistry (180), and Biology (360 — Botany + Zoology combined). Biology alone is worth as much as Physics and Chemistry put together, which should already be reshaping how you allocate your weekly hours.

Subject High-Weightage Chapters Approx. Marks/Yr
Physics Mechanics, Electrodynamics, Modern Physics, Optics ~110-120
Chemistry — Physical Equilibrium, Thermodynamics, Electrochemistry, Kinetics ~60
Chemistry — Organic GOC, Aldehydes/Ketones, Biomolecules, Haloalkanes ~60
Chemistry — Inorganic Coordination Compounds, p-Block, Periodic Table ~60
Biology Genetics, Ecology, Human Physiology, Plant Physiology, Reproduction ~200+
Takeaway: If you're forced to cut corners anywhere, cut them in Physics numericals before you cut them in NCERT Biology lines. Biology is the highest ROI subject in the entire exam.

Month-by-Month Breakdown

Months 1-4: Foundation Building

This phase is about building the raw skeleton of every chapter — not mastery, just structural familiarity. In Physics, this means getting comfortable with the mathematical tools (vectors, calculus basics, dimensional analysis) before Mechanics even properly begins. In Chemistry, Physical Chemistry fundamentals (mole concept, atomic structure) come first because they underpin everything else. In Biology, start Class 11 NCERT cover-to-cover, chapter order, no skipping.

  • Physics: Units & Measurements → Kinematics → Laws of Motion → Work-Energy-Power
  • Chemistry: Mole Concept, Atomic Structure, Chemical Bonding, and start Organic with GOC (per AKM Sir's sequencing — GOC before any named reaction)
  • Biology: Diversity in Living World, Cell Biology, Plant/Animal Kingdom — read every NCERT line, including footnotes and italicised terms

Months 5-9: Depth & Class 12 Onboarding

Once the foundation is laid, you start layering in Class 12 topics while continuing Class 11 revision cycles in parallel. This is where most students go wrong — they treat Class 11 as "done" and never touch it again until 3 months before the exam, by which point 60% of it has decayed from memory. Instead, run a rolling revision: every new month, spend 20% of your time revisiting a Class 11 chapter using only your own notes, not the textbook.

Physics

Electrostatics, Current Electricity, Magnetism, and start heavy numerical practice — 15-20 problems per topic minimum, sourced from PYQs.

Chemistry

Thermodynamics & Equilibrium (Physical), Haloalkanes/Alcohols/Aldehydes (Organic, reaction-mechanism heavy), Coordination Compounds (Inorganic).

Biology

Genetics & Evolution, Human Physiology (all systems), Reproduction — these three units alone can be 100+ marks.

Months 10-12: Syllabus Completion + First Full Revision

By this point, every chapter should have been touched at least once. This quarter is for finishing anything pending (usually Biotechnology, Ecology, or late-Organic topics like Biomolecules and Polymers), and starting your first full-syllabus revision pass — not a re-read, but active recall using flashcards, blank-sheet recall, or teaching the topic aloud to yourself.

Final 3-4 Months: PYQ Immersion & Mock Test Cycles

This is where preparation shifts from "learning" to "exam simulation." You should be taking a full-length mock at least twice a week, following strict error-log discipline (see our dedicated PYQ guide for the exact method), and re-reading NCERT Biology line-by-line at least twice more.

The Backlog Management Framework

Backlogs are not a sign of failure — they are inevitable in a syllabus this large. What separates students who recover from those who spiral is how they categorize the backlog instead of just feeling guilty about it. Use this three-bucket system every Sunday:

Bucket A — Untouched

Chapters you haven't started. Priority: schedule these first, even if it means delaying revision of completed chapters by a week.

Bucket B — Studied but Weak

You've read it once but can't solve PYQs on it. Priority: re-study with a problem-first approach, not another full re-read.

Bucket C — Solid

You can solve mixed PYQs confidently. Priority: only quick flashcard-style revision, don't over-invest time here.

The rule of thumb: never let Bucket A grow past 5 chapters at any point in the year. The moment it does, that week's schedule gets rewritten to attack it directly, even at the cost of your planned topic.

The Final Revision Month: A Different Kind of Studying

The last 30 days before NEET should look nothing like the rest of your year. No new topics. No 500-page reference books. This is a month of consolidation, and it should be built around three pillars:

  1. NCERT-only Biology revision (2 full passes). At this stage, Biology marks are won or lost purely on how well you remember exact NCERT phrasing — diagrams, exceptions, and numerical data included.
  2. Formula & reaction sheets for Physics and Chemistry. Condense your entire year of notes into single-page formula sheets per chapter, and revise only from these, not the original notebooks.
  3. One full mock every 2 days, reviewed the same evening. The goal is no longer to learn new tricks; it's to keep your test-taking rhythm — timing, guessing strategy, and stamina — sharp.
Toppers consistently report that their last-month score gains come almost entirely from fewer silly mistakes, not new knowledge. Treat the final month as a mistake-elimination sprint, not a knowledge-acquisition sprint.

Quick Checklist Before You Close This Guide

  • Have I mapped my weekly hours against the weightage table above?
  • Am I running a rolling revision of Class 11 alongside Class 12?
  • Do I categorize my backlog into Buckets A/B/C every week?
  • Have I planned my final month as revision-only, with zero new topics?

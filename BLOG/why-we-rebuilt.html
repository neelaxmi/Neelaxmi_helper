From the Founders

Why We Rebuilt Neelaxmi From Scratch

A transparent account of the incident that took everything down, and what we built in its place.

12 min read Our Story

This is not the blog post we planned to write this year. We had roadmaps for new features, new subjects, new content. Instead, this post exists because on August 28, 2025, we lost almost everything we had built — and we think our community deserves the full, honest story of what happened, why it took as long as it did to come back, and what's different now.

The Night Everything Went Dark

We won't pretend this section is easy to write. On the evening of August 28, 2025, our infrastructure suffered a cyber incident that resulted in a near-total wipe of our production data — course content, community discussions, user progress records, and portions of our internal tooling. It happened fast, and by the time our team was alerted and began responding, the damage was already done.

We made a deliberate choice early on: we would not rush out a half-explained statement, and we would not disappear silently either. We wanted to understand exactly what happened before we spoke about it publicly — and once we did understand it, we committed to telling this story straight, including the parts that don't flatter us.

The honest truth is that our old infrastructure was under-hardened for the scale we had grown to. We had prioritized shipping features fast for a fast-growing student base, and security architecture had not kept pace. That is on us, not on any single bad actor's cleverness.

The Months In Between

What followed the incident was, honestly, the hardest stretch our team has been through. There were nights spent staring at corrupted backups wondering if entire modules were gone for good. There were difficult calls with mentors whose recorded lectures had taken hours to produce and existed nowhere else. There was real grief in that — not just technical loss, but the loss of work people had poured themselves into.

What got us through it wasn't a clever engineering fix. It was the community. Students who had used Neelaxmi for months reached out — not to complain, but to ask how they could help. Some sent us screenshots and notes they'd personally saved from lectures, unknowingly becoming the only surviving copies of content we thought was gone forever. Others simply stayed patient, checking in every few weeks instead of walking away. That patience is the only reason this rebuild felt possible instead of pointless.

We want to be specific about this: the recovery of Neelaxmi was not a solo founder effort. It was a joint project between our core team and a community that refused to let a single bad night define the platform.

Building V2: What's Actually Different Now

We didn't want to simply restore what we had and hope it doesn't happen again. We used the rebuild as a forcing function to redesign our infrastructure from the ground up, with security as a first-class requirement rather than an afterthought. A few of the concrete changes in our V2 architecture:

AreaV1 (Before)V2 (Now)
BackupsPeriodic, single-locationContinuous, multi-location redundancy
Access ControlBroad internal accessStrict role-based access, least-privilege by default
MonitoringManual checksAutomated anomaly alerts around the clock
Recovery PlanInformal, undocumentedDocumented incident-response runbook, tested regularly

None of this is glamorous, and most students will never notice it directly — which is exactly the point. Good security infrastructure is invisible when it's working.

May 14, 2026: The Relaunch

We chose not to relaunch the moment things were "technically working." We wanted the platform to actually be better than before it went down — not just restored, but improved with everything we'd learned. On May 14, 2026, we brought Neelaxmi back online, rebuilt module by module, with our community testing early access builds and flagging issues before the wider relaunch.

Some content genuinely couldn't be perfectly restored, and we won't claim otherwise. But what came back is, in our honest assessment, a stronger platform — technically, and in how closely it was rebuilt alongside the people who actually use it every day.

To Everyone Who Stayed

We started Neelaxmi because we remembered what it felt like to prepare for these exams without the resources we wished we'd had. Losing the platform for those months felt like losing that mission itself. What we learned instead is that the mission was never really the servers or the software — it was the community that kept showing up around it. That's what we rebuilt around, and that's what we intend to protect far more carefully going forward.

Key Dates

  • August 28, 2025: The incident occurred and production data was wiped.
  • The months between: Recovery, rebuilding, and redesigning our infrastructure with the community's help.
  • May 14, 2026: Neelaxmi relaunched on V2 architecture.
